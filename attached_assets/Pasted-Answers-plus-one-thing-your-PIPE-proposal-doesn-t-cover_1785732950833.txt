1. RSJ 152X152X11 / X13 / X9.1 — do NOT pick between 8 mm and 9 mm. The [34.5] / [34.6]
   bracket is a genuinely different section weight and, per your own finding, a different
   thickness. Treat this the same way as PIPE: for these keys the bracket is meaningful.

   Cleanest approach: keep the existing ambiguity guard. If a stripped key maps to more than
   one thickness, do not silently pick — fall through to the next resolution level and count
   it. Then tell me how many WIP marks land on those three keys so I can size the problem.

   From the 31-Jul WIP, the relevant sections are RSJ 152X152X11 - ([37.1) (87 marks,
   1,461 MT), RSJ 152X152X13 - [37.1] (44 marks, 1,973 MT) and RSJ 152X152X9.1 - [37.1]
   (7 marks, 48 MT). All three carry [37.1] in the WIP, which maps to 8 mm unambiguously in
   the master. So if you match RSJ on the FULL string like PIPE, rather than the stripped
   key, all three resolve correctly to 8 mm and the conflict disappears entirely.

   That is my preferred answer: match on the full raw Section string first, and only fall
   back to the stripped key when the full string has no match.

2. RSJ 150X150 — use 9 mm, the RAW MATERIAL row (RM08010063). The finished-goods row
   (FG04090008, "MS RSJ 150X150") is from FG JOB WORK and should not be in the RSJ lookup
   at all. Better still: scope the thickness lookup by Group Name so FG JOB WORK rows can
   never collide with RSJ POLE rows. That removes this class of conflict rather than

Summary of what I'd change in your approach: stop stripping brackets before matching. Match
on the full Section string first (which resolves RSJ and PIPE conflicts alike), and use the
stripped key only as a fallback. And scope lookups by Group Name so raw material and finished
goods entries don't collide.
